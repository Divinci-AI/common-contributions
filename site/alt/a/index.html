What if software were kept up by everyone?

Most of the software the world runs on is open source, and most of it is looked after by a few people who are not paid enough to. AI makes changes to that software cheap to write. The open question is whether those changes can be trusted. Common AI is a bet that they can, if they stay small, capped, and in front of a person.

Not installable yet

01 · The problemMaintenance is the scarce thing.

Writing code is getting cheaper. Reading it, reviewing it and standing behind it is not. Maintainers already turn away well-meant contributions because each one costs more attention than it gives. A flood of AI-written changes would make that worse, not better.

Free software only stays healthy if a contribution costs the maintainer less than it gives.

02 · The beliefSoftware could become a commons.

If AI changes can be made safe to receive, then anyone with spare capacity could help keep a project current, and the cost of software would drift toward what is left: hosting it, looking after it, and fitting it to the way you work. We hope that means more people paid to tend and customise open source for others, and a fairer baseline for everyone.

These are hopes, not forecasts. Most maintainers are unpaid today, lower prices do not arrive by themselves, and nothing here pays anyone.

03 · The ruleThe maintainer decides.

Nothing happens to a project unless its maintainers opt in, in writing. We do not contact anyone, and a proposal on our list is a suggestion, not a listing. This is the design we are building toward; publishing a contribution is not built yet.

  1. A maintainer says yes, and says how: one file in the project names what an AI may touch.
  2. The work is small and capped. One file at a time, inside a spend limit, with a kill switch that only a person clears.
  3. Machines check first. Tests, a secret scan and a host allowlist run before anyone is asked to read anything.
  4. A person decides. The change goes up from the contributor's own account, labelled as AI-assisted.

04 · The catalogCheck before you spend a token.

We would also like a universal catalog of open software. When you ask an AI to build something, it looks there first. If a project already does the job, you use it: fewer wasted tokens, fewer copies to keep alive. If not, you build it and offer it back, so the next person finds it.

The catalog does not exist yet. It is the part we most want to see built.

05 · Where we areEarly, and honest about it.

Built: the safety checks, and a dry run of them on small tasks. Designed: how a maintainer opts a project in. Not built: an installer, the catalog, publishing a contribution, and a name we have cleared. The full status of every piece.

06 · A questionWhat would an acceptable AI contribution look like in your project?

That is what we most need to learn, and the reason nothing is installable yet.
